# Start Scene Dialogue

## Story

Tomorrow the family leaves Grandpa's house. Tonight the rooms are full of half-packed boxes, and nobody is quite ready to finish the job. Grandpa is gone, but the house still feels like his. He built the shelves. He made toy boats with the younger boy. After dinner he used to sit upstairs and play an old wooden flute, and he liked to say the notes could call an old day back.

On the wall hangs a photo from a summer festival. Grandpa stands in the middle, laughing, with the flute at his lips. Paper lanterns glow behind him. On the back of the photo, in his own handwriting, he left a line for whoever would come after him.

The flute is still upstairs. He meant it for the older child, the one the player controls. Once that child picks it up, the summer in the photo is no longer only a memory. One breath on the flute opens the past, and the journey through time begins.

Before the stairs will open, the child needs to speak with a parent, look at the photo, and talk with their brother. Talking to either parent is enough. Dad and Mom each tell the same evening in their own way, so either conversation can stand on its own.

## The family

Dad is in the middle of the books, relabeling a box he has already labeled wrong. He is practical and a little scattered, but he is the one who remembers how Grandpa built the shelves and how the flute sounded after dinner. He wants the children to look at the photo before it disappears into a box.

Mom keeps changing her mind about what should be shipped and what should stay. Plates can be replaced. Grandpa's things cannot. She is the one who says the flute has been waiting, and that Grandpa always meant it for this child.

The brother is younger. He is on the floor with a toy river and the little boats Grandpa made with him, and he intends to take every one of them to the new house. He does not want to leave. He also knows the flute is not his to pack. Grandpa told him it belongs to his sibling.

The player is the older child, moving through the house on this last night. The family speaks to them as someone old enough to be trusted with what Grandpa left behind.

Grandpa never appears in the room. He is present in the shelves, the boats, the festival photo, and the flute he set aside for the child who would come after him.

## Dad

Prompt: Talk

1. The books go in the box marked Keep, though I think I labeled it wrong again.
2. We're leaving your grandpa's house tomorrow. He built these shelves himself.
3. He used to play his flute upstairs. He said the notes could call an old day back.
4. Look at the photo before we pack it, and try not to step on your brother's river.

## Mom

Prompt: Talk

1. That box was marked Ship. Then I changed my mind.
2. We can buy new plates. We can't replace what your grandpa left behind.
3. The photo on the wall is the summer festival. He still has the flute in his hands.
4. It's waiting upstairs. He always said it should go to you.

## Brother

Prompt: Talk

1. Don't step on my river. I'm bringing all these toys when we move.
2. Grandpa made the little boats. He said this river runs all the way to the festival.
3. Mom says everything has to be in a box tonight. I don't want to leave his house.
4. The flute upstairs is his. He told me it belongs to you.

## Photo

Prompt: Look

1. A photo from a summer festival. Everyone looks younger.
2. Grandpa stands in the middle, laughing, with the flute at his lips.
3. The lanterns behind him are the same ones still sitting in the hall closet.
4. On the back, his handwriting reads: For the one who comes after.

## Stairs

Shown if the player tries to go upstairs too early.

Talk to your parents, look at the photo, and see your brother before going upstairs.

## Flute

Prompt: Take

Taking the flute ends the start scene and begins the journey through time.

1. This is Grandpa's flute. The wood is still warm, as if he just set it down.
2. The summer from the photo feels close enough to touch.
3. Take it, and you can travel through time.
4. One breath, and that old day opens.
